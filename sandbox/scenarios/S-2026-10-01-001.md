## Observation(s)

TechnologyChecker's Q3 2026 internet-outage tracker (data analyst Emma Davies), re-queried from Cloudflare Radar's verified outage annotations on 2026-10-01, logged 48 disruptions in Q3 2026 (vs. 22 in Q2, 71 in Q3 2025). Iraq was the only country with government-directed shutdowns in Q3: 18 exam-day shutdowns between July 1 and September 28 (in three bursts: July 1–8, Aug 26–Sept 10, and a special round from Sept 26 running past quarter-end), each a short morning window (e.g., 03:00–04:45 UTC). Across 2026, five countries deliberately cut access (Iraq 21, Sudan 10, Iran 2, Uganda 1, Republic of the Congo 1). Government-directed shutdowns were 18 of 48 Q3 events (38%) — the largest cause category; power failures were second at 14 (including four Cuban nationwide grid collapses). September data is provisional (Cloudflare refreshed it the same day).

## Relevant context

Government-directed internet shutdowns are a recurring, calendared administrative practice in several countries (exam integrity, elections, unrest). They are announced in advance and executed as short, bounded windows — operationally the opposite of an accidental outage.

## Why structurally interesting

The largest outage category in Q3 is not a failure at all but an *intended administrative control* — governments routinely severing national connectivity as an exam-integrity tool. A structural pattern where the "incident" is the control working as designed: recurring on a calendar, announced in advance, and invisible to risk models that only count accidents.
