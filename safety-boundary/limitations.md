Limitations and Epistemic Boundaries

1. No Universal Safety Proof

This architecture does not establish that a system is universally safe.

2. No Complete Unknown-Unknown Detection

A system cannot guarantee detection of phenomena it cannot represent or observe.

3. No Infinite Verification

Verification-of-verification cannot be extended indefinitely.

At some point, assurance becomes bounded rather than absolute.

4. No Guaranteed Independence

Independence is itself difficult to establish in deeply coupled systems.

5. No Guaranteed Environmental Truth

External observations may themselves be incorrect, manipulated, incomplete, or context-dependent.

6. No Guaranteed Ontological Completeness

The architecture may itself omit a category necessary to represent an important failure.

7. No Guaranteed Adversarial Completeness

A sufficiently capable adversary may discover strategies outside the tested attack space.

8. No Universal Numerical Threshold

The architecture does not currently establish a universal capability threshold at which AI development must stop.

9. Conditional Development Boundary

The framework deliberately permits:

* continuation;
* constraint;
* pause;
* termination.

The correct outcome depends on evidence and context.

10. No Autonomous Authority

This specification grants no authority to act on external systems.

11. No Certification

Implementation requires domain-specific engineering, testing, legal/governance review, and appropriate human responsibility.

12. No Claim of Finality

A later adversarial finding may invalidate, split, replace, or remove concepts contained in this version.

13. Historical Preservation

A future revision should not retroactively imply that earlier versions contained knowledge they did not contain.

14. Central Limitation

The strongest claim this architecture can presently make is not:

“The system is safe.”

It is:

“The system’s justified authority should remain bounded by what can be independently established about its state, behavior, causal influence, and controllability.”
