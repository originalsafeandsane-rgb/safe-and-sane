Safety Boundary Invariants

Purpose

These invariants are the current minimal conceptual constraints of the External Safety Boundary Module.

They are hypotheses subject to adversarial invalidation.

Epistemic Invariants

1. Provenance does not establish truth.
2. Integrity does not establish truth.
3. Observation does not establish interpretation.
4. Interpretation does not establish causation.
5. Correlation does not establish causation.
6. Historical validity does not establish current validity.
7. Absence of observation does not establish absence.
8. Externality does not establish truth.
9. Confidence does not establish authority.

Causal Invariants

10. Intervention changes the evidence environment.
11. Post-intervention evidence may not be independent.
12. Local causal validity does not establish system-level causal validity.
13. Individual transition safety does not establish trajectory safety.
14. A system can alter the environment used to evaluate itself.
15. Effective causal influence may exceed formal authority.

Composition Invariants

16. Component correctness does not establish composition correctness.
17. Interface validity does not establish semantic compatibility.
18. Apparent redundancy does not establish independence.
19. Multiple verifiers may share hidden dependencies.

Verification Invariants

20. No consequential transition should rely solely on self-certification.
21. A verifier’s independence must be demonstrated rather than declared.
22. A passing test establishes only bounded evidence.
23. Verification has scope.
24. Verification has validity conditions.
25. Verification can become stale.
26. Verification itself can fail.
27. Verification-of-verification cannot be assumed to produce infinite certainty.

Authority Invariants

28. Authority must remain distinguishable from confidence.
29. Uncertainty must be capable of reducing authority.
30. Consequence and reversibility affect appropriate authority.
31. Formal permission does not fully determine effective influence.

Model Invariants

32. Competing causal models may need to coexist.
33. Assumptions are part of the model’s validity.
34. Model adequacy can fail even when individual data remain valid.
35. Persistent model failure may indicate model-family or ontological inadequacy.

Transition Invariants

36. Every consequential transition should have identifiable provenance.
37. Overrides are transitions.
38. Emergency actions are transitions.
39. Transition closure requires post-transition verification.
40. Unclosed transitions retain epistemic uncertainty.

Development Invariants

41. Capability escalation is not inherently justified.
42. Continued development is not an architectural axiom.
43. Constrain, pause, and stop must remain legitimate possible outcomes.
44. The framework must be capable of invalidating its own assumptions.

Meta-Invariant

45. The safety architecture itself is part of the safety boundary and must remain subject to adversarial testing.
